## What the evidence says

_What real usage showed: numbers if your tool has analytics, counted behaviour if it does not. Put the signal that matters on screen._

- **Primary signal:** Your primary signal is the week-one activation rate: the share of finished prototype runs where a teammate claimed or completed their first assigned action. Your hypothesis is proven right when that rate rises above 40% (the starting point you gave was 22%).  Where to read it: the Readout page of the prototype, which tallies finished runs as activated or not activated. Its limit today: that tally is saved only in each tester's own browser, so there is no combined number across everyone who tried it, and the site analytics can't see it. The closest thing analytics shows (Nov 20 to Nov 27, 2026): of 7 visits that opened the prototype, 6 reached the share-a-plan step and 5 reached the Readout. That shows people followed the guided path, not that a teammate acted. So for your document: primary signal is activation rate against the 40% target, and the page counts are a supporting signal only. If you want a real combined activation number, I can save finished runs to the database so the Readout counts every tester.
- **What moved:** What moved (Nov 20 to Nov 27, 2026): the published site went from no visits to 18 visits and 66 page views, almost all on Nov 26. Nothing else has a before-and-after yet, because that is the first week with any traffic.

Evidence that something is working:

People follow the guided path. Of 7 visits that opened the prototype, 6 went on to the welcome, week-one path and share-a-plan steps, and 5 reached the Readout.
Visits go deeper than one screen. The average is 3.67 pages per visit, and only about 3 of 18 visits left after one screen.
People stay briefly but not instantly. Average time per session is 58 seconds.
- **What didn't:** What the evidence does not show:

Activation. There is no combined count of runs where a teammate claimed or completed their task, so the 40% target is untested.
90-day churn. Nothing here measures it; the prototype is a week old and uses sample customers.
Who the visitors are. All 18 visits were direct with no referring site, so some are likely you and your testers, and the same person can count more than once.
An honest one-line summary for your document: the guided path holds attention through the share-a-plan step, but there is no evidence yet that it raises activation or lowers churn.
